# Career Growth Framework

## Growth Planning Principles
Career plans should connect an individual's interests and goals with demonstrated capabilities, opportunities, and sustainable learning. Ask the person what direction they want; do not infer ambition from age, family status, background, or current title. Revisit goals as role needs and interests change.

## Career Paths
Common pathways include:
- **Individual contributor:** associate → practitioner → senior → staff/principal or domain expert.
- **People leadership:** practitioner → team lead → manager → director, with explicit people and delivery responsibilities.
- **Product or domain pathway:** technical or analytical practitioner → product/domain specialist → portfolio or strategy leader.
- **Adjacent transitions:** analyst to analytics engineering; software engineer to platform or cloud; data scientist to ML engineering; engineer to security or reliability; business analyst to product operations.

These are examples, not required ladders. Titles and scope vary by organization.

## Skill Progression
1. **Foundation:** core concepts, safe tool use, clear written explanation, and reproducible work.
2. **Independent practice:** deliver scoped work, test outcomes, document decisions, and ask for review.
3. **Ownership:** lead an end-to-end problem, manage trade-offs, and measure outcomes.
4. **Broader impact:** improve systems, mentor peers, align stakeholders, and influence standards.
5. **Strategic scope:** set direction, manage portfolio-level risk, and develop sustainable capability across teams.

Progression depends on role scope and repeated evidence, not tenure alone.

## Learning Roadmap
A practical 90-day plan:
- **Weeks 1–2:** select a target role, identify three priority gaps, and establish baseline evidence.
- **Weeks 3–6:** study one focused concept at a time; practice with small exercises and feedback.
- **Weeks 7–10:** build a role-relevant project with tests, documentation, and a measurable result.
- **Weeks 11–12:** review the work with a mentor, update portfolio/resume evidence, and plan the next cycle.

Keep goals specific and achievable; adjust for access, schedule, and support needs.

## Promotion Criteria
Agree criteria in advance: sustained performance at current scope, evidence of next-level responsibilities, quality and reliability, collaboration, judgment, and impact appropriate to role. Promotion should not depend on visibility, similarity to leaders, uncompensated work, or opaque “potential.” Provide equitable access to stretch work and feedback.

## Leadership Development
Practice clear delegation, inclusive facilitation, coaching, conflict resolution, decision records, risk communication, and accountability. Leadership can be shown through technical direction and enabling others without direct reports. Evaluate outcomes and the quality of the environment created for teammates.

## Industry Certifications
Choose certifications only when they support a target role, recognized standard, compliance need, or structured learning objective. Compare issuer credibility, exam scope, currency, cost, accessibility, and hands-on relevance. Certification is supporting evidence, not a substitute for practical capability.

## Role Transition Paths
- **Data Analyst → Data Scientist:** statistics, experimental design, modeling, validation, and communicating uncertainty.
- **Data Analyst → Analytics Engineer:** data modeling, software practices, orchestration, testing, and warehouse operations.
- **Software Engineer → ML Engineer:** model lifecycle, data and feature pipelines, deployment, monitoring, and ML evaluation.
- **Software Engineer → Cloud/Platform:** networking, identity, infrastructure as code, reliability, cost, and incident response.
- **Frontend → Full Stack:** API contracts, persistence, security, observability, and end-to-end testing.
- **Business Analyst → Product Analyst/Product Operations:** outcome metrics, experimentation, prioritization, process design, and stakeholder alignment.

Transition plans should recognize existing transferable skills and validate gaps with a small practical project.

## Examples
**Example — analyst growth:** A reporting analyst wants analytics engineering. Build a tested dimensional model and scheduled data pipeline; document data quality checks and review it with a practitioner. Use demonstrated maintainability and stakeholder outcomes as evidence.

**Example — technical leadership:** A senior engineer seeks staff scope. Lead a cross-team reliability improvement, document decisions, mentor contributors, and measure operational outcomes. Evaluate sustained influence, not just project visibility.
