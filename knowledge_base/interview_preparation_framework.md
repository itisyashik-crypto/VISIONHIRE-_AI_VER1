# Interview Preparation Framework

## Structured Interview Design
Set role outcomes, question competencies, rating anchors, time, allowed resources, and accommodation options before interviews. Ask every candidate the same core questions. Use neutral follow-ups such as “What was your contribution?” and “What would you change?” Avoid puzzles or trivia unless they closely represent the work.

## Technical Questions
Choose questions tied to real responsibilities. Assess fundamentals, correctness, trade-offs, testing, reliability, security, and explanation. For coding, define evaluation criteria and allow reasonable clarifying questions. For technical discussions, reward a clear approach and verification, not just a memorized answer.

## Behavioral Questions
Use behaviorally anchored prompts such as:
- “Tell us about a time you had to clarify an ambiguous requirement.”
- “Describe a delivery risk you identified and how you handled it.”
- “Tell us about feedback that changed your approach.”
Ask candidates to distinguish personal contribution from the team's work.

## STAR Framework
- **Situation:** relevant context, concise and confidential.
- **Task:** responsibility, goal, and constraints.
- **Action:** candidate's specific decisions and collaboration.
- **Result:** outcome, evidence, and what was learned.
Use STAR as an interview aid, not a rigid communication test. Candidates may use another clear structure or need an accommodation.

## Project-Based Questions
Explore problem, users, contribution, architecture, alternatives, testing, deployment, impact, collaboration, and lessons. Ask for enough detail to verify ownership without requesting proprietary information. A strong answer is specific and reflective, not necessarily about a large or commercial project.

## System Design Questions
State scale and constraints clearly. Assess clarification, functional and nonfunctional requirements, interfaces, data flow, architecture, failure modes, security, operations, and trade-offs. Calibrate depth to seniority and permit candidates to state assumptions.

## Scenario Questions
Use plausible role scenarios with relevant context. Assess prioritization, diagnosis, stakeholder communication, risk, and safe escalation. Do not expect knowledge of a company's internal process unless it was provided to every candidate.

## Communication Evaluation
Score role-relevant clarity, listening, organization, and decision explanation against agreed anchors. Do not score accent, speed, eye contact, charisma, or extroversion. Provide accessible formats and allow processing time or written responses where reasonable.

## Interview Readiness
Readiness means the candidate can demonstrate core role capabilities in the planned assessment, understands the format, and can discuss relevant examples. It is not confidence, polish, or a model score. Identify preparation support and unknown areas separately.

## Mock Interview Guidance
1. Select three role competencies and a short representative exercise.
2. Use the same time and evaluation criteria as the real interview.
3. Capture answers without interrupting except for neutral clarification.
4. Give feedback tied to observed behavior and one actionable next step.
5. Repeat with a different prompt to assess learning and transfer.
6. Protect real candidate and employer information.

## Common Mistakes
- Asking different core questions to different candidates.
- Leading questions, premature interruptions, or changing criteria mid-interview.
- Halo/affinity bias and anchoring on one strong or weak answer.
- Rewarding confidence or polish instead of evidence.
- Penalizing a reasonable clarification or alternative solution.
- Asking for confidential employer details.
- Treating a missing answer as proof without a fair opportunity to respond.

## Scoring Framework
Rate each competency from 1 to 5 using shared anchors: 1 materially below essential requirement after fair assessment; 2 partial; 3 meets; 4 strong; 5 exceptional; N/O not observed. Note evidence and confidence. Agree on weights before interviewing. Do not average N/O values into a score. The panel should explain the recommendation in terms of job requirements and evidence.

## Interviewer Debrief
Record independent evidence first. Discuss differences against anchors, identify unobserved areas, and agree on a follow-up if needed. Keep hiring recommendation distinct from interview readiness coaching. Candidate feedback should be respectful, specific, and consistent with policy.
