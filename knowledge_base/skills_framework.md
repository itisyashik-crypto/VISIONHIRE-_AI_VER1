# Skills Framework

## How to Use This Framework
Skill importance and market demand vary by industry, region, seniority, and date. Treat the guidance as a starting point, not a ranking of people. Confirm current role requirements and assess demonstrated evidence. A named skill is not proof of proficiency, and absence from a resume is not proof of inability.

## Programming Skills

### Python
- **Importance:** high for automation, analytics, data science, and backend services.
- **Market demand:** broad, with depth varying by role and domain.
- **Industry use:** data pipelines, APIs, scripting, ML, testing, and operations.
- **Interview relevance:** data structures where relevant, readable code, tests, error handling, complexity, and dependency choices.
- **Projects:** tested API, ETL pipeline, automation tool, or reproducible analysis.
- **Certifications:** optional; practical project evidence is usually more informative.
- **Learning path:** syntax and data structures → testing and packaging → APIs/data libraries → reliability, profiling, and deployment.

### Java and JVM
- **Importance:** high in many enterprise services and Android-related systems.
- **Market demand:** established across enterprise and platform environments.
- **Industry use:** APIs, distributed services, high-volume systems, and business applications.
- **Interview relevance:** object-oriented design, collections, concurrency, JVM behavior, testing, and service architecture.
- **Projects:** Spring service with persistence, validation, tests, and observability.
- **Certifications:** vendor credentials may help in specific ecosystems; not a substitute for delivery evidence.
- **Learning path:** language and collections → design/testing → framework and persistence → concurrency and operations.

### JavaScript / TypeScript
- **Importance:** high for web interfaces and increasingly full-stack applications.
- **Market demand:** broad in product and web teams.
- **Industry use:** browser applications, server-side APIs, tooling, and cross-platform products.
- **Interview relevance:** asynchronous behavior, type safety, browser/runtime fundamentals, accessibility, testing, and performance.
- **Projects:** accessible, tested UI integrated with a typed API.
- **Certifications:** generally optional; portfolio quality is stronger evidence.
- **Learning path:** language fundamentals → types and async → framework architecture → accessibility, performance, and tests.

### SQL
- **Importance:** high for data, analytics, and most business applications.
- **Market demand:** broad across technical and analytical roles.
- **Industry use:** querying, modeling, reporting, operational persistence, and data quality.
- **Interview relevance:** joins, aggregation, window functions, NULLs, query plans, and schema reasoning.
- **Projects:** validated reporting model, query-backed service, or documented analytical study.
- **Certifications:** platform-specific credentials can help where the platform is required.
- **Learning path:** filtering and joins → aggregation/window functions → modeling → performance and data quality.

## Data Skills

### Statistics and Experimentation
- **Importance:** high for analytical decisions and model evaluation.
- **Market demand:** strongest in data, product, research, and experimentation teams.
- **Industry use:** uncertainty, sampling, hypothesis testing, experiment design, and measurement.
- **Interview relevance:** assumptions, confidence, bias, effect size, and causal limitations.
- **Projects:** experiment analysis with a preregistered question and caveats.
- **Certifications:** formal coursework can support foundations; practical reasoning matters most.
- **Learning path:** descriptive statistics → probability → inference → experiment design and causal reasoning.

### Data Modeling and Engineering
- **Importance:** high where reliable analytics or data products are needed.
- **Market demand:** strong in organizations operating warehouses and pipelines.
- **Industry use:** ETL/ELT, dimensional models, orchestration, lineage, and quality checks.
- **Interview relevance:** idempotence, schema evolution, backfills, observability, and privacy.
- **Projects:** tested, documented pipeline with freshness and quality checks.
- **Certifications:** cloud/data-platform credentials can validate platform familiarity.
- **Learning path:** SQL and modeling → orchestration → testing and governance → scale and reliability.

### Visualization and BI
- **Importance:** high for decision communication.
- **Market demand:** broad in analytics and operations.
- **Industry use:** dashboards, metric definitions, monitoring, and stakeholder reporting.
- **Interview relevance:** chart selection, accessibility, misleading scales, and decision context.
- **Projects:** dashboard with metric definitions, validation, and user feedback.
- **Certifications:** Tableau or Microsoft Power BI credentials may be relevant to the target stack.
- **Learning path:** data quality → visual encoding → dashboard design → governance and adoption.

## AI Skills

### Machine Learning
- **Importance:** role-dependent; high where predictive systems are built or maintained.
- **Market demand:** varies by specialization and business need.
- **Industry use:** ranking, forecasting, classification, recommendations, and anomaly detection.
- **Interview relevance:** leakage, baselines, calibration, evaluation, drift, and limitations.
- **Projects:** reproducible model with representative validation and error analysis.
- **Certifications:** cloud or vendor ML credentials can support platform learning.
- **Learning path:** statistics and data → baseline models → validation → deployment, monitoring, and responsible use.

### Generative AI and Retrieval
- **Importance:** growing but use-case dependent.
- **Market demand:** varies; production engineering and evaluation are more durable than prompt-only familiarity.
- **Industry use:** grounded search, summarization, assistant workflows, and content transformation.
- **Interview relevance:** retrieval quality, grounding, citation, privacy, injection risks, evaluation, and fallbacks.
- **Projects:** RAG app with chunking, source attribution, retrieval tests, and failure handling.
- **Certifications:** choose current vendor training only when it matches the target stack.
- **Learning path:** model basics → prompting and structured outputs → retrieval/evaluation → security and monitoring.

## Cloud and Operations Skills

### Cloud Architecture
- **Importance:** high for roles deploying or operating services.
- **Market demand:** broad but vendor-specific requirements differ.
- **Industry use:** compute, storage, networking, identity, managed data, and resilience.
- **Interview relevance:** least privilege, availability, cost, observability, and recovery.
- **Projects:** deploy a small service with infrastructure as code, monitoring, and rollback.
- **Certifications:** AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud role-level credentials can support structured study.
- **Learning path:** networking and identity → core services → architecture → security, cost, and resilience.

### Containers and CI/CD
- **Importance:** high in modern software delivery and platform work.
- **Market demand:** common in DevOps, platform, and backend teams.
- **Industry use:** reproducible packaging, automated testing, release, and deployment.
- **Interview relevance:** image security, pipeline gates, rollback, secrets, and runtime debugging.
- **Projects:** tested pipeline deploying a containerized application with health checks.
- **Certifications:** Kubernetes credentials may matter for cluster-focused roles.
- **Learning path:** Linux and networking → containers → pipeline fundamentals → orchestration and operations.

## Business Skills

### Requirements and Process Analysis
- **Importance:** high in business analysis, product, operations, and consulting.
- **Market demand:** steady where complex processes need translation into delivery.
- **Industry use:** discovery, process mapping, acceptance criteria, and change analysis.
- **Interview relevance:** ambiguity, stakeholder trade-offs, traceability, and measurable outcomes.
- **Projects:** process map tied to validated requirements and acceptance tests.
- **Certifications:** IIBA or Agile credentials may be relevant to particular employers.
- **Learning path:** stakeholder discovery → modeling → prioritization → validation and change control.

### Product and Domain Judgment
- **Importance:** high for decisions affecting users or business outcomes.
- **Market demand:** cross-functional and domain dependent.
- **Industry use:** prioritization, outcome definition, risk analysis, and feedback loops.
- **Interview relevance:** rationale, alternatives, constraints, and measurement.
- **Projects:** documented decision with user evidence, success metric, and iteration.
- **Certifications:** optional; domain training may be useful where regulated.
- **Learning path:** user/problem discovery → metrics → prioritization → experimentation and strategy.

## Communication Skills

### Written and Verbal Explanation
- **Importance:** relevant across roles; required level depends on responsibilities.
- **Market demand:** universal collaboration capability.
- **Industry use:** design records, handoffs, stakeholder updates, and incident communication.
- **Interview relevance:** organization, listening, uncertainty, and adaptation to audience.
- **Projects:** concise technical proposal or presentation of analysis and limitations.
- **Certifications:** not generally necessary.
- **Learning path:** structured writing → audience awareness → facilitation → negotiation and executive summaries.

## Leadership Skills

### Collaboration, Mentoring, and Decision Ownership
- **Importance:** increases with scope but can be demonstrated at any level.
- **Market demand:** valued where work crosses teams or requires sustained ownership.
- **Industry use:** alignment, quality standards, mentoring, conflict resolution, and delivery.
- **Interview relevance:** specific actions, inclusion, accountability, and outcomes.
- **Projects:** lead a cross-functional improvement or mentor a contributor with consent.
- **Certifications:** coaching or management training can help; evidence is behavior and outcomes.
- **Learning path:** reliable ownership → feedback and facilitation → mentoring → strategy and organizational influence.
