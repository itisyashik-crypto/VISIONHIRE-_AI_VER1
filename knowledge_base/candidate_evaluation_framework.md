# Candidate Evaluation Framework

## Principles
Use structured, job-related evidence, shared rating anchors, and consistent opportunities to demonstrate capability. Score dimensions independently before discussing an overall recommendation. Record observed evidence, source, confidence, and limitations. Do not infer traits or capability from protected characteristics, resume polish, confidence style, school prestige, employer brand, or similarity to the interviewer.

## Assessment Dimensions

### Skill Assessment
Evaluate essential skills through specific examples, work samples, or structured technical discussion. Distinguish exposure, independent application, and depth. Accept equivalent tools and transferable methods where appropriate.

### Experience Assessment
Assess recency, scope, complexity, ownership, and relevance rather than years alone. Years can be a contextual feature but are not a substitute for demonstrated ability. Account for career breaks and nontraditional routes without penalty.

### Project Assessment
Examine problem framing, individual contribution, technical choices, validation, delivery, collaboration, impact, and lessons. Verify what is evidenced; ask about missing context rather than assuming.

### Communication Assessment
Where role-relevant, assess clarity, listening, explanation of trade-offs, and audience adaptation using consistent prompts. Do not equate accent, speed, extroversion, or eye contact with communication competence.

### Problem Solving
Assess how the candidate clarifies a problem, forms hypotheses, tests assumptions, considers edge cases, evaluates trade-offs, and communicates uncertainty. Reward sound reasoning even when the first answer is incomplete.

### Leadership and Collaboration
Look for actions such as alignment, constructive feedback, mentoring, conflict resolution, accountable delivery, and enabling others. Leadership can be demonstrated without a formal title or management role.

### Learning Agility
Assess evidence of feedback use, reflective practice, adaptation to changing constraints, and transfer of learning. Avoid interpreting one unfamiliar tool as inability to learn.

### Growth Potential
Evaluate plausible next-scope readiness from evidence, motivation stated by the candidate, and support needs. Clearly separate observed capability from forecast or speculation.

### Technical Depth
Assess fundamentals, architecture, implementation details, testing, reliability, security, and operational thinking appropriate to level. Use role-specific anchors, not trivia volume.

## Scoring Framework
Use a shared five-point scale:

| Rating | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Evidence was fairly sought but materially below an essential requirement |
| 2 | Partial evidence; significant support or development required |
| 3 | Meets the expected level with sufficient, role-relevant evidence |
| 4 | Strong ownership and judgment beyond baseline expectations |
| 5 | Repeated exceptional evidence at scope materially above the level |
| N/O | Not observed, inaccessible, or not assessed; do not convert to zero |

For every score, record an example, the source (resume, work sample, or interview), confidence, and any follow-up. An overall score should be reported only when enough dimensions have evidence and weights were set in advance.

## Suggested Scorecard
Adapt before candidate review; suggested initial weights are skills 30%, experience/scope 20%, projects/problem solving 20%, communication/collaboration 15%, and learning/growth 15%. Define essential versus preferred criteria for each role. State any role-specific adjustment and apply it consistently.

## Evaluation Rules
1. Ask consistent core questions and give equivalent assessment conditions.
2. Score independently before panel discussion to reduce anchoring.
3. Cite evidence; separate observation from interpretation.
4. Treat missing or parser-omitted data as unknown.
5. Resolve contradictory evidence with a relevant follow-up.
6. Do not penalize accommodation use, communication difference, or nontraditional experience.
7. Keep model outputs separate from human ratings; explain their limitations.
8. Use no protected or irrelevant personal attributes.
9. Preserve a reasoned human decision and documented override.

## Ranking Rules
Rank only candidates evaluated against the same role criteria and comparable stages. Compare dimension-level evidence before any weighted aggregate. Avoid false precision from close totals. If evidence coverage differs, complete the missing assessment or mark the comparison inconclusive. Historical selection probability supports review only; it does not overrule direct evidence.

## Examples
**Example 1 — skill transfer:** The job asks for one cloud vendor; the candidate demonstrates equivalent identity, networking, and deployment concepts on another. Record demonstrated transfer and assess vendor-specific gaps separately.

**Example 2 — absent evidence:** A resume does not state team size. Mark leadership scope not observed and ask a common collaboration question. Do not infer “no leadership.”

**Example 3 — conflicting ratings:** One interviewer rates a design answer highly while another sees a reliability gap. Compare notes to the shared reliability anchor and conduct a focused follow-up; do not average unexplained impressions.
